Finding-Level Fixation Attribution in Chest Radiography

Comparing learned selection with structured baselines

A completed radiologist scanpath mixes evidence for multiple reported findings. We ask how much finding-specific localization can be recovered by explicit structure—and what remains for a learned fixation selector.

Result in one line

Structured references approach learned overlap; the learned difference is clearer for peak placement, modest overall, and heterogeneous across findings.

Main evidence · n = 987

Strong references change the conclusion

A temporal reference leaves a large apparent gain. Adding training-only anatomy, target-record scanpath support, directional terms, and temporal gating closes most of it. The 3.0-second row is the strongest tested overlap reference, not a claimed optimum.

Peak placement retains a visible learned difference. Seed-0 learned minus the 3.0-s structured reference: +0.0405, 95% patient-cluster interval +0.0108 to +0.0696.

Not one uniform effect

Learning helps some findings and not others

The learned-minus-structured difference changes sign across finding categories. Use the control to switch metrics; positive bars favor learned selection.

The learned advantage depends on training scale

With 10–50% of training patients, structured overlap remains competitive or higher. Learned overlap exceeds it only with the full training set in this sensitivity analysis.

Interpretation

What the evidence supports

01

Baseline design matters

Temporal-only comparisons overstate what learning adds when anatomy, scanpath coverage, and directional language are available.

02

Peak and extent differ

The strongest paired comparison supports a peak-placement difference but does not resolve an IoU difference.

03

Records are not interchangeable

Coarsely matched other-patient records perform worse than target records; this is evidence of non-interchangeability, not cognitive-intent recovery.

Scope boundary

The selector is ellipse-supervised and uses no radiograph pixels. The study does not establish annotation savings, online deployment, causal cue contributions, or downstream image-model utility.
